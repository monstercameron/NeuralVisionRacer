# LightGaussian: global significance pruning and SH distillation

**Decision: prototype conservative pruning; defer SH distillation and VecTree quantization.** This is the most directly relevant published path to fewer resident Gaussians and therefore less WebGPU sorting, raster work, overdraw, and per-splat upload. Its published best-case numbers are not transferable to this renderer, and the research code carries a non-commercial upstream license.

## Technique and evidence

LightGaussian (Fan et al., NeurIPS 2024 Spotlight; arXiv:2311.17245) scores each Gaussian's global contribution over training views. It combines hit count with opacity and Gaussian volume to identify low-significance splats, removes a chosen fraction, then co-adapts/retrains the retained model to recover quality. Its pipeline also distills directional appearance from higher-order spherical harmonics (SH) to lower degree using synthesized pseudo-views, then applies significance-aware VecTree quantization to attribute vectors. These are distinct steps: pruning reduces count and runtime work; SH reduction/quantization primarily reduce representation bandwidth and storage. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17245), [official project page](https://lightgaussian.github.io/), [official repository README](https://github.com/VITA-Group/LightGaussian/blob/main/README.md).

The authors report average compression above 15× and FPS increasing from 144 to 237 in their 3D-GS evaluation over Mip-NeRF 360 and Tanks & Temples. The project page also reports 139→215 FPS, illustrating that the headline depends on the reported run/version. A detailed paper result summarized in the paper's benchmark reports 144→237 FPS and 782→45 MB on an NVIDIA A6000, with PSNR 27.40→27.13, SSIM .813→.806 and LPIPS .217→.237. These are author results on their CUDA 3DGS implementation and dataset/view setup; no browser, WebGPU, game, car-animation, or 1280×720 performance claim follows from them. At a single Room-scene ablation, the paper shows pruning alone causes a visible quality drop and co-adaptation recovers much of it. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17245), [project page](https://lightgaussian.github.io/).

The official README says direct pruning starts from a trained 3D-GS model, with a 20,000-iteration checkpoint and a prune/fine-tune pass; its default prune+recovery result is about 35% of original PLY size at comparable quality. It exposes separate prune/recovery, SH distillation, and quantization commands, and notes a modified `diff-gaussian-rasterization` submodule for significance measurement. The GitHub `main` branch has no tagged release, so `main` is a mutable source reference rather than a reproducible source pin. [README](https://github.com/VITA-Group/LightGaussian/blob/main/README.md).

## Fit to NeuralVisionRacer

The current repository has already applied SOG v2 WebP streams, per-stream 256-entry codebooks, shared coordinate ranges, SH centroid labels/palettes, CPU decode staging and a bounded/reused WebGPU resident pool. The optimization notes say nominal GPU stream-texture savings are already 48.9 MB for Saburi and 73.1 MB for Delft, with upload payload 28 rather than 36 bytes per splat. The remaining stated major costs are splat count, projected coverage and transparency overdraw. Pruning is therefore complementary to existing SOG compression: each removed splat avoids stream bytes and work, rather than re-encoding the same attributes more compactly. [performance notes](../../performance.md), [SOG v2 decoder](../../../crates/splat-render/src/sog.rs), [streaming pool](../../../crates/splat-render/src/stream.rs).

For current assets, build an offline converter around the decoded SOG bundle and the existing WebGPU renderer's output semantics. A training-view significance score could be estimated by replaying representative cameras and accumulating each splat's contribution, but that is an adaptation of the paper, not its tested method. Existing SOG bundles do not, by themselves, provide the original camera images or optimization state required for the published co-adaptation/recovery and SH distillation. Original captures/COLMAP camera data would make faithful reproduction possible. Without them, holdout rendered views from game cameras can support an engineering proxy, but not ground-truth fidelity claims. Keep all optimization offline; runtime remains portable WebGPU.

Pruning preserves each retained splat's original opacity, center, covariance and SH unless the recovery stage changes them. That is attractive for the moving car: wheel indices/deformation and wheel/body SH clearcoat and shadow behavior can remain intact if records are pruned without reordering or the auxiliary indices are remapped exactly. However, the score is global and view-frequency dependent. Small but high-contrast wheel spokes, trim, tire edges, body silhouette, thin mirrors/antennae and occlusion/shadow contributors can have low aggregate contribution. Splat-level deletion may punch holes or alter alpha-compositing and shadows even if average PSNR looks acceptable. Freeze all wheel-linked splats in the first experiment, then separately test a small body/environment pruning ratio. Do not use a blanket opacity or scale threshold as a substitute for the method.

SH distillation is especially risky for the car's view-dependent clearcoat and reflected highlights; treating SH truncation as a free memory win would change appearance. VecTree quantization has limited incremental value where SOG has already quantized values to byte labels and compressed those label textures with WebP. It could instead add conversion/decode costs and a new runtime representation. Test pruning alone before considering the other stages.

The existing stream LOD leaves, selection budgets, allocator and crossfades can consume reduced-count LOD variants without architectural changes if each variant retains leaf/range metadata and all associated per-splat indices. Generate independent offline-pruned variants per existing LOD rather than baking pruning only into LOD 0. Use the current stable order and fades; validate wheel shadow and transparency under live view movement. Smaller payloads should reduce load/upload pressure. Resident-pool capacity is currently based on a fraction of LOD0, so whether to lower capacity should be decided from measured occupancy and memory, not assumed: the renderer already avoids duplicate means textures and bounds residency.

## Risks, licensing and unknowns

- The paper's metric is global contribution over its training cameras. A racing camera has a different and highly nonuniform distribution; unseen corners, reverse driving, close-up replay/photo modes, and low-angle wheel views may expose pruned regions.
- Repeated alpha compositing is nonlinear. Importance from one reference model cannot guarantee that the remaining splats reproduce the same color, opacity, or depth order after removal.
- No published LightGaussian result covers SOG v2, quantized source streams, deformed subassemblies, our SH lighting model, or a WebGPU renderer. Recovery training with a CUDA rasterizer could optimize for different raster semantics.
- The official LightGaussian code's `LICENSE.md` is the inherited GraphDeco 3DGS license: research/evaluation, non-commercial use only. The repository says it is based on 3DGS and uses a modified rasterizer submodule. Do not copy or ship that code in this game without a separate rights review. The paper's algorithm may be reimplemented independently, but that does not resolve rights in source assets or third-party code. [License](https://github.com/VITA-Group/LightGaussian/blob/main/LICENSE.md), [README](https://github.com/VITA-Group/LightGaussian/blob/main/README.md).
- The project page and paper abstract quote different baseline/result FPS, and all are author-side CUDA numbers. Treat them as motivation, not a forecast.

## Smallest useful prototype and gates

Create a separate, disposable offline converter that accepts one original SOG v2 chunk, reconstructs Gaussian parameters and stable IDs, replays a fixed camera set, computes approximate hit/opacity/volume importance, and writes pruned SOG variants at two mild ratios (for example 10% and 20% removed). Preserve wheel-linked records exactly. This initial proxy can run without training data, but report it explicitly as a LightGaussian-inspired score, not a reproduction. If original capture cameras/images are available, then add recovery fine-tuning as a second experiment. Do not edit the game runtime until results pass.

Use held-out Saburi and Delft camera paths with a stopped car, driving turns, near/far approaches, low wheel views, and reverse views. Compare full-resolution reference and candidate at the same viewport and effects. Gate on no visible car/wheel silhouette or clearcoat/shadow regression; no new holes/flicker while driving; SSIM/LPIPS plus pixel residuals on held-out frames; unchanged deformation/index correspondence; and real-browser 1280×720 WebGPU timings for sort, depth, color and total GPU frame, upload bytes, resident occupancy, decode and cold-load peaks. Require consistent repeated improvement in end-to-end browser GPU time or memory/load targets without lowering resolution, detail, LOD defaults, or effects. Don't compare against published CUDA FPS.
