# Technique report: hierarchy-merged Gaussian LODs

**Source technique:** *A Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Representation for Real-Time Rendering of Very Large Datasets* (Kerbl et al., ACM TOG 43(4), 2024; SIGGRAPH 2024). Official project and code: [project page](https://repo-sam.inria.fr/fungraph/hierarchical-3d-gaussians/), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12080), [official implementation](https://github.com/graphdeco-inria/hierarchical-3d-gaussians). Reviewed 2026-10-26; code link points at `main`, so exact source commit was not pinned in this review.

## What it does

Builds a spatial hierarchy of ordinary 3D Gaussian primitives. Leaves are the trained fine scene; internal nodes are computed by merging children into a Gaussian whose mean/covariance approximate the weighted mixture. Appearance coefficients use contribution weights. The merge weights account for opacity and projected Gaussian area. Internal-node opacity is treated as *falloff* and may exceed one, then alpha is clamped in rendering. A view-dependent hierarchy cut chooses a level by projected screen-space granularity, not simply fixed world distance. During refinement, child attributes interpolate from the parent; child alpha is initialized with `1 - (1 - alpha_parent)^(1/K)` so K overlapping children initially reproduce parent alpha. Intermediate nodes can be further optimized against full-resolution views across randomized target granularities; a compaction pass removes rarely useful internal nodes. These are offline representation/training steps, not a shader-only switch.

Primary descriptions: [hierarchy generation, merge formulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12080) (Secs. 4.1–4.2), [interior-node optimization and compaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12080) (Sec. 5), [official project summary](https://repo-sam.inria.fr/fungraph/hierarchical-3d-gaussians/).

## Performance evidence and its limits

The paper's rendering metrics were collected on an NVIDIA A6000. Its single-chunk quality/FPS comparison reports that for the coarser `tau2` cut the hierarchy gets 106, 107, 79, and 101 FPS on SmallCity, Wayve, Campus, and BigCity, compared with leaf-only 58, 70, 51, and 39 FPS. A still coarser `tau3` reaches 157/125/106/128 FPS but is visibly lower quality before parent optimization; optimized `tau3` improves image quality while reporting 159/135/104/137 FPS. The paper also reports LOD table quality metrics PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS. These are CUDA/reference-renderer results on the authors' prepared scenes and a single-chunk evaluation; they are not comparable directly to this browser's WebGPU game frame times, and do not establish a speedup for SOG assets or the existing renderer. [Paper evaluation tables and hardware context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12080).

Large-scene creation costs are material: 4/11/22/46 chunks for SmallCity/Wayve/Campus/BigCity; each chunk contains 2–8M leaves. Coarse, chunk and hierarchy optimization each took about an hour on one 32 GB V100; peak memory for hierarchy optimization is reported as 11–16 GB. Final disk models were 6/17/27/88 GB, about 68% larger than non-hierarchical 3DGS. The whole-scene artifact notes include capture sparsity and residual moving-object artifacts. This is a compelling asset-authoring result for kilometer captures, but a substantial offline pipeline and storage increase for today's 2.35M/5.15M resident SOG levels. [Paper large-scene results](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12080).

## Fit against NeuralVisionRacer today

The engine already stores spatial leaves with independently authored ranges at each SOG LOD, selects per leaf by screen-space spacing/error (default 1.5 px at 1080 reference height), frustum/distance, shared fixed pool budget, hysteresis/keep delay, crossfades, prefetch, and per-frame upload cap. `stream.rs` already does global pool drawing and a global depth sort; `lod.rs` reads the tree's bounds and per-LOD chunk offsets/counts. Therefore hierarchy traversal, leaf selection, streaming, and transitions are not new here. The plausible novelty is **better parent Gaussian content**: synthesize/optimize intermediate-level splats to represent a child set with materially fewer splats and less image error, and perhaps derive a target screen-space cut more faithfully than spacing heuristics. That would be an offline content-pipeline experiment, not adoption of their renderer.

Current levels use SOG V2 and custom quantization/codebooks/SH palettes. Reusing leaves is feasible in principle by reading/decompressing them and emitting parent nodes as another ordinary Gaussian LOD, but exact compatibility is unproven. The paper's falloff can exceed 1 and relies on alpha-clamp semantics; standard quantized SOG opacity may not encode this. An exporter could clamp/bake falloff, or renderer/format changes could preserve its semantics, either with quality risk. Merged covariance must be represented by the current orientation/scale fields; merged SH must survive palette/codebook quantization. There is no need to retrain the existing leaf scene to test raw merging, but optimized parents require source images/cameras and a CUDA differentiable training stack, and cannot be reproduced in WGSL. The paper uses full-resolution images during parent optimization and Mip-Splatting EWA filtering; source assets/training views are not known to be available for the packaged levels.

The state/car model is a separate animated high-detail entity. Keep it outside the static scene hierarchy and global coarsening policy; vehicle body/wheels and wheel deformation need their present detail. A dynamic cut can still use static world camera position while leaving car splats fixed at their current LOD. Crossfading parent and children temporarily increases resident/drawn splats and global-sort input, potentially hurting the GPU-bound frame; the already high coverage/alpha work means fewer candidates alone may not save time if merged Gaussians grow in screen footprint. Pool pressure, bytes, upload amount, sort count and overdraw all need measurement.

## Portability, licensing, open questions

The hierarchy math itself (tree cut, merge, screen-space granularity, interpolation) is renderer-independent and could be implemented in Rust/WGSL/WebGPU. The reference training and rasterizer are CUDA/PyTorch; neither their timing nor CUDA code transfers to browser WebGPU. Any new per-node attributes and alpha blending must stay within current WGSL storage limits. The SOG falloff representation, exact internal-node alpha-clamp equivalence, and image quality of post-quantized merged nodes are open questions. The project paper says code is free for non-commercial research/evaluation; verify exact current `LICENSE.md`, the upstream 3DGS license, and project/game asset rights before using or redistributing code/checkpoints. [Official source code](https://github.com/graphdeco-inria/hierarchical-3d-gaussians).

## Smallest useful prototype and gates

1. Offline, write a throwaway converter for one small static SOG leaf/chunk set. Merge siblings by weighted mean/covariance/SH, emit a conventional SOG coarse level, and compare against the existing authored coarse level. Initially avoid falloff >1 by using the renderer's current opacity semantics; separately record where that compromise clips.
2. Keep the production tree selector, pool, animation and fades unchanged. Add the generated level as an alternate coarse asset and run the saved 1280x720 browser comparisons on fixed camera positions/slow orbit and transient zooms, keeping car, effects, resolution and quality unchanged.
3. Gate on image error/visual review against the original render (PSNR/SSIM plus explicit thin detail/edge checks; no silent detail lowering), at least 20% fewer visible splats at matched image quality in the tested far views, and a repeated browser-WebGPU GPU-time win larger than paired-run noise. Record active splats, resident bytes/upload, sort cost, frame p50/p95, transition peaks and coverage. If it only improves residency/storage or fails GPU gate, do not claim an FPS gain.

## Recommendation

**Prototype only as an offline parent-LOD asset generator**, because conventional merged Gaussians could improve the existing multi-LOD assets without replacing the streaming architecture. First test compatibility and quality on one small SOG region. Defer differentiable parent optimization until source camera/image training data and an acceptable offline GPU workflow are confirmed. Do not adopt the CUDA viewer or assume its FPS applies here.
